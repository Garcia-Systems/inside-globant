# Chapter 24 — Getting into the Room

An enterprise meeting is permission to discuss a consequential problem, not merely a calendar invitation. The buyer may expose strategy, architecture, operational weakness or regulated data. Access therefore reflects relevance and trust.

Public evidence shows several doors, but not their relative contribution. Globant has long-standing customers, public industry and Studio positions, offices in customer markets, acquisitions intended to add capabilities and market presence, customer/brand partnerships such as Formula 1 and FIFA, and technology ecosystems such as Google Cloud and AWS ([About](https://www.globant.com/about), [FIFA](https://www.fifa.com/about-fifa/commercial/media-releases/fifa-and-globant-announce-multi-year-partnership), [Google Cloud announcement](https://www.globant.com/news/globant-expands-partnership-google-cloud), [AWS partner profile](https://partners.amazonaws.com/partners/0010h00001h3VppAAE/Globant)). These establish routes to visibility and relationships—not the origin of any confidential lead.

## What the evidence permits

* **Documented fact:** Globant bought WAE while explicitly describing strategy/design and stronger US presence as rationales ([acquisition announcement](https://www.globant.com/news/globant-acquires-leading-digital-strategy-and-design-firm)). An acquisition can bring people, reputation and relationships as well as revenue.
* **Documented fact:** named multiyear relationships and old accounts give Globant recurring contact surfaces.
* **Reasonable inference:** successful work can expose adjacent needs and sponsors; a platform partner may introduce an implementation need.
* **Unknown:** referral rates, outbound/inbound mix, event conversion, lead attribution and the entry route for each published customer.

## General industry routes—not a Globant funnel

| Route | Why a meeting becomes plausible | Evidence a buyer may request |
|---|---|---|
| existing account | context and supplier onboarding already exist | delivery history and adjacent relevance |
| executive or practitioner relationship | a trusted person recognizes a problem | current capability, not friendship alone |
| partner ecosystem | platform adoption creates implementation work | certification, references, architecture depth |
| acquisition | acquired firm already has local relationships | continuity and combined capability |
| thought leadership/event | insight makes a problem discussable | specificity beyond marketing |
| referral | another party transfers some trust | comparable outcome and conflicts check |
| targeted outreach/inbound inquiry | problem and capability appear to match | evidence that warrants deeper access |

These are **general industry models**. The record does not support a percentage allocation or a mandatory route. At enterprise scale, “getting in” often requires multiple proofs: recognizable relevance, credible people, a reason to act, and confidence that disclosure is safe. Access is the beginning of qualification, not evidence that work will be awarded.

## Questions to Think About

1. Which route transfers the most trust, and which merely creates awareness?
2. How can sponsorship and services coexist without proving which originated the deal?
3. What should an acquired relationship be worth if delivery continuity fails?
