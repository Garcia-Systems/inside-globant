# Chapter 34 — From Solution to Commercial Proposal

A technical solution becomes purchasable only when it becomes a set of commitments:

```text
scope + deliverables + team + timeline + assumptions
       + price + responsibilities + terms
```

This is where engineering becomes commerce. Globant's FY2024 filing says its service arrangements include time-and-materials and fixed-price contracts and discusses their different revenue recognition and risk implications ([FY2024 Form 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1557860&owner=exclude)). That supports those categories at company level, not the form of a named customer contract.

## Commercial forms

| Model | General allocation logic | Globant status in this chapter |
|---|---|---|
| time and materials | customer pays for agreed effort/rates; prioritization remains active | filing-supported category |
| fixed price | price tied to defined commitment; provider bears more estimation/scope risk | filing-supported category |
| managed team | provider organizes a continuing team/capacity | **general industry model**; do not assume a standard Globant label |
| managed service | provider accepts continuing service responsibilities/levels | general industry model |
| milestone-based | commercial events align to accepted phases/outputs | general industry model |
| outcome-oriented | payment/risk partly tied to measured outcome | general industry model |

No model automatically produces alignment. T&M can accommodate learning but reward unmanaged consumption. Fixed price can encourage clarity but also defensive scope or change disputes. Outcome arrangements require attributable measures and control over the drivers. Hybrids are possible, but public evidence does not justify assigning them to Globant accounts.

A proposal should distinguish deliverables from hoped-for business outcomes. Software can be delivered; adoption and revenue may depend on the customer's operations, marketing, decisions and market. The document must also preserve architecture assumptions, acceptance, customer duties, intellectual property, security and change mechanics for later handoff.

**Unknown:** Globant rate cards, discount authority, proposal templates, margin thresholds, approval chain and contract-level mix beyond disclosures. Refusing to fill those gaps is part of understanding the commercial system accurately.

## Questions to Think About

1. Which outcome can the provider actually control?
2. How can a proposal preserve discovery uncertainty?
3. Why is the cheapest comparable rate not necessarily the cheapest system?
