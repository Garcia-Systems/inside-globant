# Chapter 40 — What Part III Teaches Us

Engineering capability becomes revenue only through a chain of customer permission and provider commitment. Market presence creates consideration; relationships create access; business development finds and develops relevant demand; account leadership preserves context; industry expertise identifies business meaning; consulting and technical specialists define options; delivery leadership tests feasibility; commercial/legal infrastructure makes commitments buyable; delivery creates the evidence for trust and possible repeat work.

No reviewed source establishes one Globant funnel or a single role that owns this chain. The strongest evidence is architectural: Globant publicly has clients, commercial expense, industries, Studios, global delivery, consulting/engineering services, contracts, partner relationships and long-lived accounts ([FY2024 Form 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1557860&owner=exclude), [Services](https://www.globant.com/services), [Industries](https://www.globant.com/industries), [Studios](https://www.globant.com/studios)). Public roles add evidence of customer, commercial and technical functions, but not a universal organization chart.

## Answers from the evidence

* **Relationships are valuable** because trust and retained context reduce the cost of consequential conversation; they do not guarantee a purchase.
* **Business development contributes** targeted learning, relationships and opportunity formation—not merely meeting volume.
* **Account leadership contributes** continuity and coordination across customer, commercial and delivery concerns.
* **Industry expertise contributes** the operating and economic context that makes the right problem visible.
* **Technical expertise enters early enough** to test claims, then progressively deepens as commitment rises.
* **The bridge is a function, not necessarily one title:** consultants, architects, technical/Studio leaders, account people and delivery leaders may translate between business and engineering.
* **Delivery feasibility matters during sales** because a services promise is a promise about future people and coordinated work.
* **Partnerships matter** because platforms create customer-specific implementation needs and ecosystems can transfer capability signals or access.
* **The first project matters** because delivered behavior is reusable commercial evidence and retained context may expose the next real problem.

## What remains unknown

The public record does not establish Globant's quotas, commissions, global stage vocabulary, qualification framework, proposal approvals, pricing rules, staffing reservations, account decision rights, handoff standard, partner lead attribution, win rates or contract-level margins. Job postings cannot fill those gaps, and case studies usually begin after the concealed buying decision.

## The major conclusion

If a company already possesses talented engineers somewhere in the world, it still needs capabilities to turn those engineers into US client revenue:

1. **reputation and access** sufficient to enter a high-trust conversation;
2. **commercial and industry judgment** to recognize a valuable, fundable problem;
3. **customer-facing technical translation** to connect business consequences with architecture, constraints and options;
4. **solution and estimation discipline** to convert ambiguity into bounded commitments;
5. **global mobilization and delivery leadership** to make the proposed team real;
6. **security, legal, procurement and financial infrastructure** to become an acceptable enterprise supplier;
7. **reliable delivery and account stewardship** to produce outcomes, preserve trust and earn the chance to solve another problem.

```text
CUSTOMER PROBLEM ↔ RELATIONSHIP ↔ COMMERCIAL UNDERSTANDING
↔ TECHNICAL UNDERSTANDING ↔ SOLUTION ↔ DELIVERY CAPABILITY
↔ SIGNED ENGAGEMENT ↔ BUSINESS OUTCOME
```

A technically credible person who can move accurately in both directions across that chain creates commercial value by improving the decision before code begins: clarifying need, exposing risk, mobilizing the correct expertise, preventing infeasible promises and making engineering realities meaningful to the buyer. That person is not “extra overhead.” The function is part of the mechanism by which engineering becomes revenue.
