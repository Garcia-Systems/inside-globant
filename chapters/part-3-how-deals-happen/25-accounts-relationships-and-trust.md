# Chapter 25 — Accounts, Relationships, and Trust

**TRUST IS COMMERCIAL INFRASTRUCTURE.**

Globant's FY2024 filing describes long-standing client relationships, names prominent customers and reports customer counts at revenue thresholds and concentration among its largest accounts ([FY2024 Form 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1557860&owner=exclude)). The EA relationship is described as older than a decade; public announcements call several sports relationships multiyear. [Chapter 21](../part-2-customers/21-from-project-to-strategic-account.md) explains why this is portfolio evidence of durability, not a private account ledger.

An account is more than a CRM record. It can hold knowledge of stakeholders, systems, decision rhythms, controls, commercial history and delivery behavior. That accumulated context reduces the cost of asking the next useful question. It can also create concentration and dependency risk; the filing explicitly treats customer demand and concentration as risks.

## What must be trusted

The customer is not only judging a salesperson. A major initiative can require confidence in:

* technical competence and candor about constraints;
* delivery capacity, continuity and governance;
* security, confidentiality and data handling;
* organizational and financial stability;
* communication across geography and time zones;
* industry judgment and regulatory awareness;
* commercial reliability when assumptions change.

Trust is not the absence of controls. Sophisticated trust is supported by references, architecture review, security evidence, contract terms, governance, transparent estimates and observed delivery. A strong relationship may get a provider heard; it should not excuse weak feasibility.

**Reasonable inference:** repeated successful work lowers perceived execution and coordination risk, making adjacent conversations easier. **Unknown:** which Globant sponsor owns a particular relationship, whether an account is exclusive, why any extension occurred, or how satisfaction is measured internally.

Trust also flows in both directions. The provider depends on customer access, decisions, truthful constraints and payment. A feasible proposal must state customer responsibilities rather than treating cooperation as free. This is why relationship health and delivery health are inseparable.

## Questions to Think About

1. Which forms of trust can evidence create, and which require experience?
2. When does retained account context become dependency?
3. Why can governance strengthen rather than signal distrust?
