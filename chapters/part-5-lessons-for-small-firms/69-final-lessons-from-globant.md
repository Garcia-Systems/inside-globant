# 69 — Final Lessons from Globant

The book began with a question: **How does a global technology-services company turn distributed engineering capability into valuable engagements with major businesses?**

Globant's public record supports a richer answer than “developers around the world.” Its [FY2024 filing](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1557860&owner=exclude) documents global delivery, large client relationships, contract forms, commercial expense and operating risks. Customer and partner evidence in Parts II and IV documents selected relationships and ecosystem breadth, while leaving private deal choreography, prices, staffing and attribution unknown.

## Five accumulated answers

1. **Globant:** a commercial and technical system combining market presence, industry and specialist capability, solution work, global delivery and account continuity—not merely labor arbitrage.
2. **Customers:** buy varied combinations of judgment, capacity, implementation, integration, experience and change; public stories rarely expose the complete causal or commercial record.
3. **Deals:** require access, trust, discovery, translation, feasibility, a buyable proposal, enterprise approval, delivery continuity and earned expansion.
4. **Partners:** make platforms, credentials, complementary capability and routes available, but do not guarantee demand or success.
5. **Small firms:** can reproduce focused capabilities and controls, not Globant's scale. Their missing layer differs by proposition and customer.

A more precise economic model is:

```text
qualified demand + credible access + problem evidence
+ feasible solution + mutually acceptable commitments
+ controlled delivery + customer adoption/contribution
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                 realized business outcome
```

Engineering capacity is necessary when engineering must be done, but it is insufficient: a buyer must discover the provider, connect capability to an important problem, accept dependency, approve terms and participate in change. Sales ability is also insufficient: a relationship cannot make an infeasible architecture feasible, mobilize unavailable people, secure data, or rescue ungoverned delivery.

The valuable professional function lies in connecting a **real business problem** to **real technical capability** through evidence and controlled commitments so that both parties can produce a **real business outcome**. The function may be concentrated in one experienced founder or distributed across an account leader, consultant, architect, specialists and delivery lead. Physical location and title are secondary to capability, authority and feedback loops.

## Three roles, carefully distinguished

**SOFTWARE ENGINEER** — builds and operates systems, while potentially contributing discovery, estimates and customer communication.

**SALESPERSON** — creates and advances commercial relationships, while potentially developing domain or technical depth.

**SALES / SOLUTIONS ENGINEERING FUNCTION** — connects business requirements, technical reality and the commercial decision, whether performed by one title or a team.

No boundary is absolute. The durable control is that customer promises reach technical and delivery reality, and technical realities return in language the customer can decide. That entire layer explains why abundant global talent does not automatically create American purchases—and why trustworthy connection is itself valuable work.

## Final evidentiary boundary

This is an analytical reconstruction from public sources, not Globant's confidential playbook, a claim that all services firms work alike, or a guarantee that an independent representative or any other model will succeed.
