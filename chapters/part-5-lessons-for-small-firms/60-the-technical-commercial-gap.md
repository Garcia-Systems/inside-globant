# Chapter 60 — The Technical–Commercial Gap

Two valid languages meet in a technology engagement:

| Customer world | Engineering world |
|---|---|
| revenue, cost, operations, customers, deadlines, growth, risk | APIs, data, architecture, cloud, security, queues, integrations, debt, testing, deployment |

```text
CUSTOMER WORLD
      ↕
technical–commercial translation
      ↕
ENGINEERING WORLD
```

Translation is bidirectional. “Reduce four-day reconciliation” must become questions about source systems, matching rules, exception queues, identity, volumes, controls and observability. “Eventual consistency and poor source identifiers” must return as choices the buyer can govern: which exceptions remain manual, what error tolerance is acceptable, which source owner must participate, and whether the deadline permits data remediation.

## The function

The bridge must be able to:

* understand the problem, stakeholders and economic consequence;
* ask enough technical questions to expose dependencies and unknowns;
* understand specialists without impersonating expertise;
* compare approaches and explain tradeoffs in decision language;
* connect scope and estimates to evidence and assumptions;
* communicate delivery, security and commercial risk;
* build confidence through clarity rather than certainty theater;
* stop impossible promises and return changed terms for technical review.

This is not merely “explaining technology simply.” It shapes what is sold. The function protects both sides when it makes unpriced uncertainty, customer responsibilities, and feasibility visible before commitment.

## Titles are unreliable maps

Sales engineer, solutions engineer, solutions architect, presales consultant, technical consultant, technically deep client partner, and technical account leader may perform portions of the function. In some firms a founder and architect pair do it; in others discovery consultants, industry leaders, commercial owners and delivery specialists form a team. Globant's public [careers](https://career.globant.com/) and capability pages support the existence of customer, commercial, consulting and architecture roles, not a universal title or choreography.

A salesperson may excel at relationships and process without designing systems. An architect may excel at systems without qualifying authority, value or commercial consequence. Either can learn across the boundary; neither title guarantees it. The control is a **closed loop**: commercial owner preserves the customer decision, technical owner validates feasibility, and delivery owner validates mobilization and governance.

At small scale, one person can facilitate this loop but should not become an unreviewed oracle. Require engineering sign-off on material assumptions and commercial confirmation when design changes price or terms. Measure the function by qualified decisions, promise quality and delivery continuity—not demonstrations or meetings alone.

Whether this bridge must be physically local is the next question in [Chapter 61](61-the-local-presence-question.md).

## Questions to Think About

1. Which assumptions in the the technical–commercial gap model would need evidence before a smaller firm acted on it?
2. Which capability should remain accountable inside the engineering firm, even if some work is external?
3. What customer evidence would show that this approach is reducing risk rather than merely adding activity?
