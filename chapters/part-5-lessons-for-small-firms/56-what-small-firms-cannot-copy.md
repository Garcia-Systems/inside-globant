# Chapter 56 — What Small Firms Cannot Copy

Globant's scale is factual, although the value of each advantage in a particular pursuit is contextual. Its FY2024 [Form 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1557860&owner=exclude) documents global operations, acquisitions, large-client relationships, financial resources and enterprise risks. A 25- or 100-person firm cannot honestly simulate that resilience.

| Scale advantage | Why direct imitation fails | Smaller strategic substitute |
|---|---|---|
| global brand | recognition compounds through years, spend and visible work | niche reputation among a defined buyer set |
| huge references | access and program scale cannot be fabricated | specific, referenceable outcomes at comparable complexity |
| thousands of specialists | bench breadth requires utilization and recruiting machinery | narrow depth plus disclosed specialist network |
| acquisition budget | buying practices and market presence consumes capital and integration skill | alliances, selective hiring, organic learning |
| office network | leases do not create trust or useful coverage | travel and accountable coverage where needed |
| enterprise procurement infrastructure | legal, security, finance and insurance capacity has fixed costs | prepared evidence pack and qualified advisers |
| strategic vendor portfolio | top-tier alliances require certifications, pipeline and delivery proof | one relevant ecosystem relationship |
| enormous staffing capacity | large mobilizations require recruiting, bench and governance | bounded work sized to real capacity |
| balance-sheet reassurance | audited scale and liquidity cannot be claimed away | staged commitment, insurance, escrow or other agreed controls |
| mass marketing | broad attention is costly and noisy | concentrated insight and relationship-led access |

These substitutes are not equivalent guarantees. A niche reputation may win a specialized project but not satisfy a bank's concentration or continuity threshold. A subcontractor network may add reach while adding confidentiality, quality, worker-classification and accountability questions. A pilot can reduce exposure while failing to test production scale.

The strategic error is **scale cosplay**: publishing every fashionable capability, promising any team size, renting an address as proof of presence, or displaying credentials detached from practitioners. The credible alternative is bounded strength. State maximum mobilization, name the leaders, show relevant evidence, disclose dependencies, and decline work beyond the control system.

Scale also changes economics. Enterprise diligence and proposals can cost the same whether a provider has 50 or 50,000 people, but the smaller firm spreads that fixed cost over fewer opportunities. It should therefore qualify harder, reuse truthful diligence materials, and choose deals where distinct expertise offsets buyer concern.

That concern begins with market access, examined in [Chapter 57](57-the-us-market-gap.md).

## Questions to Think About

1. Which assumptions in the what small firms cannot copy model would need evidence before a smaller firm acted on it?
2. Which capability should remain accountable inside the engineering firm, even if some work is external?
3. What customer evidence would show that this approach is reducing risk rather than merely adding activity?
