# 61 — The Local Presence Question

A U.S. address is neither necessary for every deal nor sufficient for trust. “Local presence” bundles distinct benefits that should be tested separately.

| Needed benefit | Possible mechanism besides an office |
|---|---|
| working-hour availability | designed overlap, rotating coverage, nearshore schedule |
| business/industry fluency | experienced account/domain lead anywhere |
| relationship access | founder network, referral, vendor partner, focused events |
| onsite discovery | planned travel or customer-paid workshop |
| accountability | named owner, response commitments, escalation governance |
| procurement convenience | appropriate contracting entity and advisers |
| trust | references, evidence, communication and staged commitment |

Remote collaboration is plainly viable in many contexts: the [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/) continues to report telework, while digital-services trade is documented by the [WTO](https://www.wto.org/english/res_e/booksp_e/dtd2023_e.pdf). Neither source proves a buyer will accept remote sales or delivery for a particular regulated, classified, hardware-dependent, executive-intensive, or troubled engagement.

Physical proximity is more valuable when work depends on observation, workshops across senior stakeholders, restricted environments, equipment, or rapid crisis response. It is less valuable when the buyer has mature remote practices, specifications are stable, systems are remotely accessible, and governance is explicit. Time-zone overlap matters most for work with frequent unresolved dependencies; asynchronous work can be advantageous for focused execution if decisions and handoffs are designed.

A useful question is not “Do we need an American?” but:

> Which moments require synchronous access, contextual fluency, onsite observation, contractual presence, or a trusted accountable person—and how will each be supplied?

Possible answers include fully remote selling; periodic founder travel; a U.S.-based employee; an independent representative; distributed account and delivery leadership; or an office when demand justifies its fixed cost. Immigration, tax, employment, corporate-registration and contracting implications require qualified advisers and are outside this book.

Local presence is thus an operating design choice. The independent-partner option deserves careful examination, not automatic endorsement, in [Chapter 62](62-the-independent-sales-engineering-model.md).
