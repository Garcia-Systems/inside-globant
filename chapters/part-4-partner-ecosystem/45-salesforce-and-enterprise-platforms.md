# 45 — Salesforce and Enterprise Platforms

Salesforce is the primary case because Globant's capability is documented through an acquisition rather than inferred from a partner logo. In 2021 Globant announced its [acquisition of CloudShift](https://www.globant.com/news/globant-acquires-cloudshift), describing CloudShift as a Salesforce partner and explaining that the acquisition would strengthen Globant's Salesforce capabilities and UK presence. That is **DOCUMENTED FACT** about the transaction and stated rationale. Current tier, headcount, pipeline, and realized acquisition synergies require current evidence and are not assumed.

## Buying a platform can create work

An enterprise platform replaces some custom product construction with configurable capability. It also becomes a new center of process, data, permissions, and integration.

**GENERAL INDUSTRY MODEL**

```text
BUY SOFTWARE
     ↓
CONFIGURE roles, objects, rules, and workflows
     ↓
CUSTOMIZE only where justified
     ↓
INTEGRATE identity, finance, service, marketing, and legacy systems
     ↓
MIGRATE and reconcile data
     ↓
CHANGE processes, controls, measures, and responsibilities
     ↓
TRAIN / OPERATE
     ↓
CONTINUOUSLY IMPROVE as products and the business change
```

Thus “buy rather than build” does not mean “no engineering or consulting.” It changes the work. A team may write less commodity CRM functionality while doing more configuration, integration, data engineering, testing, release management, and organizational change.

## The Salesforce case

Customer relationship management connects sales, service, marketing, customer data, and workflows. A buyer may want a common customer view, faster service, better pipeline discipline, or new digital journeys. The platform supplies reusable objects and services. The implementation must decide definitions, access, process, migration, interfaces, and adoption.

| Layer | Typical customer question | Possible services work |
|---|---|---|
| Process | How should a lead, case, or customer journey work? | Discovery and process design |
| Platform | What should be standard versus customized? | Architecture and configuration |
| Data | Which record is authoritative and clean enough? | Mapping, cleansing, migration, governance |
| Integration | How does CRM connect to the existing estate? | APIs, events, middleware, testing |
| Change | Will employees use the redesigned workflow? | Training, rollout, measurement, improvement |

These are possible categories, not claims that every Globant Salesforce engagement contains each one.

## Complementary businesses—and a governance risk

Salesforce benefits when customers adopt and renew the platform. Globant can benefit from implementation and improvement demand. The customer benefits if the combination delivers a process outcome faster or with less risk than assembling capability alone.

But more customization and services are not inherently better. Excessive customization can raise upgrade cost; poor data can undermine adoption; unclear ownership can allow vendor and integrator to blame each other. The buyer needs a responsibility matrix, architecture guardrails, acceptance criteria, and a value case.

Adobe, SAP, Oracle, and ServiceNow represent related enterprise-platform logic where a verified Globant relationship exists (Chapter 47). They are not interchangeable: experience platforms, ERP, databases/cloud, and workflow platforms create different implementation problems.

## Evidence boundary

CloudShift provides credible historical evidence of a deliberate Salesforce capability investment. **REASONABLE INFERENCE:** acquiring an established specialist can add trained practitioners, references, and platform knowledge faster than organic hiring alone. **UNKNOWN:** current Salesforce tier, private commercial terms, referral flow, partner-sourced revenue, and the profitability of this work.

## Questions to Think About

1. What should remain standard to preserve upgradeability?
2. Is the system implementation failing, or is an unresolved process decision being exposed?
3. Who owns data quality after migration?
