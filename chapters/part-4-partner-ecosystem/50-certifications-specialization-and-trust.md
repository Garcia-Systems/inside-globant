# Chapter 50 — Certifications, Specialization, and Trust

Enterprise buyers cannot directly observe all of a supplier's skill before awarding work. Credentials compress some evidence into signals that procurement and technical evaluators can inspect.

## What credentials can mean

**GENERAL INDUSTRY MODEL.** Program terminology differs by vendor:

* a **certification** commonly attaches to an individual who passed defined requirements;
* a **competency**, **specialization**, or **designation** commonly attaches to a company after satisfying program criteria that may include trained people, references, technical validation, or performance;
* a **partner tier** groups firms under a vendor's current program rules.

AWS's [Globant profile](https://partners.amazonaws.com/partners/0010h00001h3VppAAE/Globant), Google Cloud's [partner entry](https://cloud.google.com/find-a-partner/partner/globant), and the vendor directories linked in Chapter 47 provide externally maintained evidence paths. They should be checked at the time of procurement because names, thresholds, scope, and status change.

## Commercial value

Credentials may signal:

1. baseline technical knowledge;
2. experience reviewed under vendor criteria;
3. investment in trained personnel;
4. access to specific partner programs or support;
5. specialization relevant to a workload or industry; and
6. vendor confidence sufficient for a published designation.

That can reduce search and verification cost. It can help Globant qualify for an opportunity, help a vendor identify plausible delivery capacity, and help a customer defend a shortlist. This connects directly to Part III: trust is accumulated evidence under uncertainty, and technical presales must turn a corporate badge into a credible plan.

## What credentials cannot prove

A credential does not guarantee that the certified people are assigned, that architecture fits the customer, that estimates are sound, that communication works, or that delivery will succeed. Counts can include people outside the relevant geography or practice and can change through hiring and attrition.

```text
PARTNER STATUS
      ↓ reduces one kind of uncertainty
SHORTLIST / DUE DILIGENCE
      ↓ still required
NAMED TEAM + REFERENCES + ARCHITECTURE + CONTRACT + GOVERNANCE
```

The buyer should ask for credential identifiers where appropriate, current verification, named-team availability, comparable references, responsibility boundaries, and evidence tied to the actual workload. A broad cloud tier is weaker evidence for a specialized migration than a relevant designation plus comparable delivery.

## Why Globant invests

**REASONABLE INFERENCE.** Training and partner status can make Globant's technical capacity more visible, improve platform fluency, and open program eligibility. Specialization also helps the commercial organization locate the right experts during discovery. These advantages explain a rational investment without claiming that a badge causes revenue.

**UNKNOWN:** Globant's complete current certification inventory across vendors, utilization of certified people, internal cost and return, deals won because of status, and program benefits not publicly disclosed.

## Questions to Think About

1. Is the credential current, relevant, and attached to the proposed team?
2. Which delivery risk does it reduce—and which risks remain untouched?
3. Could a vendor credential bias architecture toward that vendor?
