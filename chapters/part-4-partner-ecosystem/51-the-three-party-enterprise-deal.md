# 51 — The Three-Party Enterprise Deal

> **HYPOTHETICAL — NOT A GLOBANT CUSTOMER CASE.** The scenario combines the general industry mechanics and public capability categories discussed in this Part. It does not claim that Globant, a vendor, or a customer follows this exact process.

A U.S. enterprise wants to modernize customer-service intake. It is considering a major cloud/AI platform and Globant as consulting and implementation provider. The customer wants safer, faster resolution; the vendor wants appropriate platform adoption; Globant wants a feasible services engagement and successful delivery.

## Stage-by-stage model

| Stage | Customer goal | Vendor goal | Globant goal | Technical question | Commercial question | Principal risk | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Business problem | Reduce wait and rework without lowering safety | Understand potential platform fit | Qualify a valuable, solvable problem | Which journeys and systems cause delay? | Is there an owner, budget, and decision path? | Starting with technology rather than outcome | Problem statement, baseline, sponsor |
| 2. Platform options | Compare build, buy, and do nothing | Demonstrate differentiated capabilities | Advise credibly and identify service needs | Can options satisfy security, integration, latency, and portability? | Who influences and who decides? | Biased or premature selection | Options and selection criteria |
| 3. Partner alignment | Confirm parties can work together | Confirm implementation capacity | Access product expertise and clarify roles | Who owns model, platform, application, and data layers? | Separate contracts, prime, or marketplace route? | Responsibility gaps | Preliminary RACI and route |
| 4. Discovery | Expose process, data, control, and user needs | Refine likely product usage | Shape feasible scope with global specialists | What data is authoritative and permissible? | Is discovery paid; what follows it? | Hidden legacy and regulatory constraints | Findings, use cases, constraints |
| 5. Architecture | Obtain secure, operable design | Ensure supported use of platform | Produce implementable solution and delivery plan | Retrieval, identity, integrations, observability, human review? | What assumptions affect price and platform consumption? | Lock-in, weak controls, underestimated nonfunctional work | Architecture and decision record |
| 6. Proof of concept | Test the highest uncertainty cheaply | Demonstrate capability and learn usage | Validate feasibility without disguising a demo as production | What evaluation set and pass threshold matter? | Who funds it and owns its assets? | Cherry-picked demo or leaked data | Evidence, failure analysis, go/no-go |
| 7. Commercial design | Know total cost and accountability | Contract platform/license/consumption | Contract services at manageable risk | Are environments, support, and service levels sufficient? | Price form, credits, liability, IP, change control? | Two contracts leave a gap or duplicate cost | Business case, proposals, negotiated responsibilities |
| 8. Platform purchase | Secure required technology on acceptable terms | Win and retain platform usage | Preserve implementation schedule and access | What capacity, regions, and product terms are needed? | Commit versus consume; exit and renewal terms? | Overcommitment before value proof | Platform agreement / order |
| 9. Services engagement | Secure accountable delivery capacity | See capable implementation mobilize | Establish scope, team, economics, and governance | Which named roles and acceptance tests? | T&M, fixed price, milestones, dependencies? | Scope ambiguity and unavailable specialists | Statement of work and mobilization plan |
| 10. Build and integration | Obtain usable workflow | Support correct product use | Deliver increments and manage change | How do AI, CRM, identity, knowledge, and legacy services connect? | Who pays for discovered change and consumption? | Security defects, unstable interfaces, cost drift | Tested increments and traceability |
| 11. Deployment | Adopt safely with operational readiness | Reach production use | Achieve acceptance and protect relationship | Rollback, monitoring, support, training, and incident response? | What triggers acceptance and ongoing support? | Users bypass system; ownership unclear | Production release and operating model |
| 12. Expansion | Improve measured outcome | Grow justified usage | Win justified follow-on services | Which results support the next use case? | Recompete, extend, or stop? | Expansion by momentum rather than evidence | Benefits review and next decision |

## Different interests, one outcome

The customer optimizes business value and risk, not platform consumption or billable hours. The vendor benefits from durable adoption. Globant benefits from valuable, deliverable scope and account trust. Alignment occurs when a useful implementation increases adoption and earns services fees because it creates customer value.

Misalignment is possible at every stage. The vendor can oversell capability, the integrator can over-scope work, and the customer can withhold decisions or data while expecting a fixed result. Explicit outputs, acceptance measures, and responsibility boundaries make the three-party structure governable.

## Questions to Think About

1. At which stage should the customer be able to stop without losing bargaining power?
2. Who bears the risk that the model works but users do not adopt the workflow?
3. Which decisions must remain with the customer even when vendors provide expertise?
