# Chapter 48 — How Platforms Create Services Work

A software product and a consulting service can be substitutes at one layer and complements at another. A managed database substitutes for building database machinery. It may simultaneously create work to redesign schemas, migrate data, integrate applications, secure access, and operate the new estate.

## The adoption chain

**GENERAL INDUSTRY MODEL**

```text
PLATFORM ADOPTION
       ↓
IMPLEMENTATION NEED — establish environments, configuration, identity, controls
       ↓
INTEGRATION NEED — connect applications, data, events, and people
       ↓
CUSTOMIZATION — cover justified gaps without defeating upgradeability
       ↓
MIGRATION — move and reconcile workloads and information
       ↓
PROCESS CHANGE — alter decisions, controls, roles, and measures
       ↓
TRAINING / GOVERNANCE — make use safe, repeatable, and owned
       ↓
ONGOING SERVICES — operate, optimize, extend, and respond to vendor change
```

Not every adoption traverses every stage, and not every stage is outsourced. Most importantly, no platform purchase automatically creates Globant revenue.

## Three examples

### If AWS gains a customer

Possible work includes application-portfolio assessment, landing-zone architecture, workload migration, cloud-native modernization, security, data platforms, cost governance, and operations. AWS can receive consumption; Globant can compete for fees; the customer pays both only if the combined value case warrants it. Chapter 42 documents the relationship, not automatic attachment.

### If Salesforce expands inside an enterprise

New business units, channels, data, and workflows can require configuration, integration, migration, testing, release management, training, and continued improvement. Platform functionality reduces the need to invent commodity CRM features; enterprise fit creates different technical work. Chapter 45 shows why standardization and services can grow together.

### If an enterprise adopts generative AI

Model access introduces questions about use-case value, protected data, retrieval, evaluations, human review, security, latency, cost, monitoring, and governance. A prototype can be easier than a dependable operating process. Chapter 46 locates services demand in that implementation gap.

## The services revenue mechanism

**REASONABLE INFERENCE based on verified capabilities, not a disclosed Globant formula.** Globant can create revenue around technology it did not invent by selling scarce labor, reusable knowledge, delivery accountability, and customer-specific change. Revenue may be associated with discovery, a project, team capacity, managed service, or another contract form discussed in Parts I and III. The product vendor retains its own product economics.

The amount of work depends on complexity, legacy estate, regulation, internal capability, ambition, and make-or-buy choices. Mature standard deployment may need little outside help. A global redesign across fragmented systems may need much more.

## Complementarity has limits

Partners can overengineer, customize unnecessarily, or encourage consumption. Customers can fragment responsibility between vendor and integrator. Good governance therefore asks:

* Which outcome and metric justify the change?
* What product capability can be used as standard?
* Which custom work creates differentiated value?
* Who owns architecture, data, security, acceptance, and operation?
* What is the exit or portability plan?

The commercial insight is not “technology always produces services.” It is narrower and stronger: standardized technology leaves customer-specific work, and services firms can earn revenue when they credibly win and perform that work.

## Questions to Think About

1. Where did the platform remove work, and where did it relocate work?
2. What portion of ongoing service is value-adding improvement versus avoidable complexity?
3. How would the buyer measure implementation success independently of platform consumption?
