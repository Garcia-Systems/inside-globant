## Recurring patterns

First, the technology category predicts the implementation problem. Adobe and commercetools cluster around experiences and commerce; SAP and Oracle around enterprise processes and data; ServiceNow around workflow; Atlassian around technical and team operations.

Second, relationships connect Globant's horizontal engineering capacity to product-specific knowledge. The platform vocabulary, extension model, data objects, release practices, and certifications can be as important as general coding ability.

Third, acquisition can be an ecosystem strategy. CloudShift brought an existing Salesforce specialization. **REASONABLE INFERENCE:** buying a specialist can accelerate access to scarce credentials and references. It does not establish that integration succeeded or that historical status remains current.

Fourth, vendor confirmation varies. A searchable vendor directory is useful current evidence but can change. A dated joint announcement is stable historical evidence but may no longer describe current status. The strongest conclusion comes from both, with a verification date.

## What the table does not show

It does not rank partners, estimate revenue, assert active co-selling, or claim that a customer example involved joint selling. **UNKNOWN:** private incentives, referral volumes, resale margins, certified-person counts, current geographic scope, and partner-attributed pipeline unless a cited source explicitly discloses them.

## Questions to Think About

1. Is a buyer selecting a platform first and then an implementer, or designing the outcome first?
2. How portable are Globant's accelerators and customer data if the platform changes?
3. Which evidence is more decision-useful: tier, named references, or the proposed team's experience?
