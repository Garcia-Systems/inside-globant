# 41 — The Ecosystem Around Globant

Parts I–III followed Globant from capabilities to customers and then through the commercial–technical–delivery chain. That chain is incomplete unless it includes the technology on which much delivery depends. Globant creates software and some products of its own, but it does not manufacture AWS regions, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, Salesforce, or NVIDIA processors.

## Product company and services company are different businesses

**GENERAL INDUSTRY MODEL**

| Actor | Primarily creates | Economic objective | Persistent problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technology product company | A repeatable platform, model, application, or infrastructure service | License, subscription, or consumption growth and retention | A standard product does not implement itself in each customer's estate |
| Technology services company | Customer-specific diagnosis, design, engineering, integration, migration, and operation | Fees for expertise, outcomes, or delivery capacity | It needs credible technologies, skills, and routes to demand |
| Customer | Business outcomes using people, process, data, and technology | Better revenue, cost, risk, service, or mission performance | It must connect a product to a particular organization and legacy estate |

The categories can overlap. A vendor can sell professional services; a services firm can create accelerators or software. The useful distinction is the economic center of gravity, not a permanent label.

```text
                 requirements, adoption, consumption
TECHNOLOGY VENDOR <-------------------------------> CUSTOMER
        ^                                               ^
        | product knowledge, programs                   | context, authority,
        |                                               | acceptance
        v                                               v
                    SERVICES PARTNER
             discovery / design / implementation
```

Modern enterprise change often requires all three because the platform is deliberately reusable while the customer's data, controls, workflows, interfaces, and people are specific. Access to technology therefore answers “what capabilities are available?” It does not fully answer “what should this enterprise do, how will it fit, and who will safely deliver it?”

## A vocabulary without inflated claims

* A **software vendor** or **independent software vendor (ISV)** produces software for multiple customers.
* A **cloud provider** supplies elastic infrastructure and managed technology services. The largest are often called **hyperscalers**.
* An **AI platform** supplies models, compute, tooling, or managed AI services.
* An **enterprise platform** provides a configurable system around which processes and extensions are built.
* A **consulting partner** advises on choices and operating change. An **implementation partner** configures, builds, integrates, migrates, and deploys. One firm can do both.
* A **systems integrator** connects products and existing systems into a working whole. A **solution provider** is a broader, less precise term.

“Partner” alone establishes none of those scopes. It may describe directory membership, trained staff, an accreditation, a jointly marketed offering, resale, or a deeper strategic agreement. This Part uses precise evidence and leaves private economics **UNKNOWN**.

## What each party wants

A platform vendor generally wants adoption, successful use, expansion, and lower failure risk. A services company wants relevant demand, delivery revenue, product knowledge, credibility, and sometimes access to joint routes to market. A customer wants capability, fit, accountability, speed, and controlled risk. Interests overlap, but are not identical: a vendor may prefer greater platform consumption; an integrator may prefer more service scope; a customer wants only the cost and complexity justified by value.

That tension is why governance, architecture, transparent responsibilities, and commercial disclosure matter. The ecosystem is not automatically efficient or unbiased. It is a way of combining standardized technology with customer-specific work.

## Evidence boundary

Globant publicly documents relationships with AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft, Salesforce, NVIDIA, OpenAI and other vendors in the sources used in the following chapters. A named relationship is a **DOCUMENTED FACT** only to the scope and date of its source. It does not prove referrals, revenue sharing, partner-attributed revenue, or participation in every customer project. Those remain **UNKNOWN** unless expressly stated.
