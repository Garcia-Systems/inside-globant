# 52 — What Part IV Teaches Us

The global technology-services market is not simply **customer → developers**. The evidence and models in this Part support a richer explanation:

```text
                BUSINESS NEED
                     ↓
                   BUYER
                  ↙     ↘
                 ↕       ↕
      TECHNOLOGY VENDOR ↔ SERVICES COMPANY
                 ↘       ↙
                  SOLUTION
                     ↓
              GLOBAL DELIVERY
                     ↓
              BUSINESS OUTCOME
```

## The answers

**Why does Globant need technology partners?** Customers already depend on cloud, enterprise, data, and AI platforms. Verified relationships can give Globant product knowledge, externally legible specialization, joint solution inputs, and possible routes into platform-centered demand. Globant can then earn fees for customer-specific work around technology it did not invent.

**Why do technology companies need firms like Globant?** A repeatable product cannot contain every customer's process, data, integration, control, and adoption decision. Services capacity can help customers reach successful use, which can support vendor adoption and consumption. The sources document alliances; their private economic effect remains unknown.

**Why do customers need implementation expertise?** Buying capability does not establish architecture, migrate information, integrate the estate, redesign work, govern risk, or create adoption. Customers may supply that expertise internally, obtain it from Globant or another firm, or combine them. The need does not imply Globant automatically wins.

**How can partnerships generate opportunities?** Direct introductions, partner directories, joint offerings, marketplace procurement, vendor-assisted demand, and Globant-assisted platform selection are possible routes. The public record establishes organized relationships and discoverability, not lead volume or partner-sourced revenue.

**How does specialization increase credibility?** Certifications, competencies, and focused practices reduce one part of buyer uncertainty by making training and reviewed experience visible. They do not guarantee that the proposed team or solution will succeed. Part III's discovery, architecture, scoping, and trust mechanisms remain necessary.

**Why can a platform sale create services revenue?** Standard software removes some invention while exposing configuration, integration, migration, process, governance, and operating work. Services and products are therefore frequently complementary. They are not universally complementary: simple adoption, internal capability, or a poor value case can mean little or no external work.

**How does AI change the opportunity?** AI lowers the cost of accessing powerful general capabilities while increasing attention to use-case selection, enterprise data, evaluation, security, human oversight, integration, monitoring, and adoption. Model access makes implementation possible; it does not make implementation successful.

**How does the ecosystem expand routes into a customer?** Globant can enter through a direct relationship, a vendor ecosystem, or joint activity. It can also bring a platform into an existing account. More routes enlarge the opportunity surface while adding incentives and responsibility boundaries that the customer must govern.

## Documented, inferred, general, unknown

* **DOCUMENTED FACT:** official Globant and vendor sources establish material relationships across AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft, Salesforce, NVIDIA, OpenAI, and selected enterprise platforms.
* **REASONABLE INFERENCE:** these investments can improve capability, credibility, and access to platform-related work.
* **GENERAL INDUSTRY MODEL:** successful platform adoption can create implementation services and increase vendor usage.
* **UNKNOWN:** alliance-attributed revenue, lead flow, referral payments, margin, private commitments, and the causal effect of credentials on wins.

## The next question

Globant has global brand recognition, thousands of employees, major customers, specialized Studios, established partnerships, delivery infrastructure, enterprise credibility, commercial teams, and industry expertise.

The broader market observation is not limited to companies of Globant's scale. Many offshore firms, nearshore companies, and specialist engineering boutiques may have genuine delivery capability without the same U.S. commercial system.

That creates the question for Part V:

> **WHAT HAPPENS WHEN A COMPANY HAS TECHNICAL DELIVERY CAPABILITY—BUT DOES NOT HAVE GLOBANT'S U.S. COMMERCIAL MACHINE?**

Part IV does not answer it. [Part V](../part-5-lessons-for-small-firms/53-shrinking-the-globant-model.md) tests which elements are essential, scalable down, scale-dependent, irrelevant, or potentially supplied by an external partner.

## Questions to Think About

1. Which ecosystem capability depends on scale and which depends only on focus?
2. Can a small specialist become more credible through depth in one platform without becoming captive to it?
3. Who supplies discovery, architecture, and local trust when the delivery firm does not yet have them?
