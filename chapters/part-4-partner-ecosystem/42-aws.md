# Chapter 42 — AWS

AWS is the clearest example of infrastructure creating an adjacent services market. AWS supplies cloud services; a customer still has to assess applications, design a landing zone, move data and workloads, rewrite or retire systems, establish security and cost controls, and change operations.

## What the public record supports

**DOCUMENTED FACT.** AWS maintains an [AWS Partner Network profile for Globant](https://partners.amazonaws.com/partners/0010h00001h3VppAAE/Globant). The profile is stronger evidence than a logo because it is vendor-side recognition. Globant also describes an [AWS relationship and capabilities](https://www.globant.com/partners/aws). These pages support a current technology-and-services relationship at the time reviewed; individual badges and counts remain time-sensitive.

**DOCUMENTED FACT.** In 2023 the companies announced a [multi-year strategic collaboration agreement](https://www.globant.com/news/globant-and-aws-expand-collaboration) directed at cloud adoption and generative AI. The announcement is evidence of intended investment and collaboration, not evidence that every target was achieved. AWS also lists Globant offerings in its partner ecosystem; marketplace availability can create a procurement route, but a listing does not prove a transaction.

The source set supports work categories including migration and modernization, data and analytics, cloud-native development, and AI. It does not support a claim that Globant implements every AWS service, that AWS supplies a fixed volume of leads, or that either party shares revenue.

## The complementary economics

**GENERAL INDUSTRY MODEL, applied cautiously.** If a customer successfully moves workloads to AWS, AWS may gain consumption and retention. Globant can earn services revenue for the customer-specific work surrounding that adoption. The customer gains access to AWS technology and a delivery organization able to connect it to business and legacy context.

```text
AWS capability purchased
        ↓
portfolio assessment → architecture → migration / modernization
        ↓
security + data + integration + operating-model change
        ↓
workloads adopted and improved
```

This is complementarity, not an automatic commission. More AWS adoption can **potentially** enlarge the addressable implementation problem. Whether Globant wins it depends on customer choice, qualification, scope, competition, price, and delivery credibility.

## What each party can gain

| Party | Potential value | Evidence limit |
|---|---|---|
| AWS | More customers able to adopt, migrate, and operate AWS successfully | Public sources do not quantify Globant-attributed AWS consumption |
| Globant | Platform expertise, credentials, joint solution investment, and implementation demand | No disclosed referral fee, margin, pipeline, or partner revenue |
| Customer | Product capability plus assessment, engineering, migration, and change capacity | Partner status is not a delivery guarantee |

Competencies and certifications are useful because cloud failure can arise from architecture, skills, security, cost, or operating practices—not merely missing infrastructure. They make training and reviewed experience more legible during procurement. Yet a badge cannot substitute for references, named personnel, a sound design, and contractual accountability.

## Co-selling: say only what is known

The strategic-collaboration language and partner profile establish organized collaboration. They make joint market activity plausible. **UNKNOWN:** which accounts were introduced by AWS, how opportunities are registered, whether funds or credits were used, the commercial split, and how much Globant revenue resulted. Calling all collaboration “co-selling” would overstate the record.

## Questions to Think About

1. Does a migration replicate an old estate or use cloud adoption to redesign it?
2. Who owns cloud cost, security, and operational outcomes after deployment?
3. What evidence beyond partner tier should a buyer request from the proposed team?
