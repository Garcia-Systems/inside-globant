# Chapter 46 — AI: NVIDIA, OpenAI, and the New Ecosystem

AI makes the product–services distinction especially visible. A model endpoint, accelerator, or managed AI service can be obtained quickly. A safe, useful production system still requires choices and work around it.

## Verify the relationships, not the fashionable names

**DOCUMENTED FACT.** NVIDIA lists Globant in its [partner network](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/about-nvidia/partners/partner-locator/) and Globant describes its [NVIDIA alliance](https://www.globant.com/partners/nvidia), including work around accelerated computing and enterprise AI. The directory and Globant page verify the relationship at review time; they do not prove that NVIDIA participates in every Globant AI project.

**DOCUMENTED FACT.** Globant and OpenAI publicly announced a [collaboration](https://www.globant.com/news/globant-and-openai-announce-strategic-collaboration) focused on enterprise AI adoption. OpenAI's own [services-partner announcement](https://openai.com/index/globant-collaboration/) supplies partner-side confirmation. The exact program label and scope should be read as dated statements, not extrapolated to undisclosed commercial terms.

AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft also supply AI services and have verified Globant relationships (Chapters 42–44). A single solution can therefore involve overlapping model, compute, cloud, and application ecosystems. This book does not assume one exclusive stack.

## Access is not implementation

**GENERAL INDUSTRY MODEL**

```text
ACCESS TO MODEL / COMPUTE / AI SERVICE
                  ≠
SUCCESSFUL BUSINESS IMPLEMENTATION
```

The gap can include:

1. selecting a valuable and permissible use case;
2. defining authoritative, lawful, and sufficiently clean data;
3. threat modeling, identity, privacy, and access control;
4. application and legacy-system integration;
5. workflow and human-approval redesign;
6. evaluation for quality, safety, bias, latency, and cost;
7. governance, documentation, and escalation;
8. deployment, observation, incident handling, and change control; and
9. training, adoption, and measurement of business outcomes.

A technology vendor can improve models and tools. A services company can apply them to a particular enterprise. The customer must set purpose, risk appetite, and decision rights.

## The emerging economics

An AI platform company benefits when experiments become durable production usage rather than abandoned demonstrations. Globant can sell discovery, data, design, engineering, evaluation, integration, governance, and managed improvement. The customer can obtain specialized capacity without expecting a model provider to redesign its complete operation.

**REASONABLE INFERENCE:** Globant's multi-vendor relationships allow it to address customers whose cloud and AI estates differ, and formal alliances may reduce the cost of demonstrating platform familiarity. They can also create conflicts: the easiest partner technology to sell may not be the best customer choice. Multi-vendor capability does not itself prove neutrality.

## Evidence from Globant initiatives—and its limit

Globant's public [AI services](https://www.globant.com/artificial-intelligence) and alliance pages document an investment in AI-oriented offerings. They support capability positioning, not universal client results. Terms such as “agentic” or “AI transformation” describe a direction unless a named case supplies baseline, intervention, and measured outcome.

**UNKNOWN:** OpenAI- or NVIDIA-attributed Globant revenue, referral mechanics, model discounts, compute commitments, exclusivity, and how liability is allocated in private customer contracts.

## Questions to Think About

1. What test would prevent a compelling demonstration from becoming an unsafe deployment?
2. Who owns evaluation data and the authority to stop the system?
3. Does the proposed architecture preserve the customer's ability to change models or platforms?
