# Chapter 12 — Google: Customer, Partner, or Both?

Google is important precisely because the public record is easy to overread. Globant's corporate history identifies Google as an early client, and its FY2024 filing names Google among customers served. Separately, Globant and Google Cloud maintain a technology-ecosystem relationship. Those propositions can both be true; they are not interchangeable ([Globant About](https://www.globant.com/about), [FY2024 Form 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1557860&owner=exclude), [Google Cloud partner expansion](https://www.globant.com/news/globant-expands-partnership-google-cloud)).

## What is documented

**Documented fact:** Globant says Google became a customer early in Globant's history. The annual filing uses Google as a customer example, but public materials reviewed for this edition do not provide a project-level narrative, system architecture, team, KPI, or contract chronology. This supports a relationship claim, not a reconstruction of what Google bought.

**Documented fact:** the Google Cloud relationship concerns Globant helping *other organizations* adopt and use Google's cloud technology. Globant has announced expanded collaboration and Google Cloud maintains a partner ecosystem through which service firms can develop competencies and deliver implementations ([Globant announcement](https://www.globant.com/news/globant-expands-partnership-google-cloud), [Google Cloud partners](https://cloud.google.com/partners)). In that channel Google can be platform vendor and ecosystem partner while an enterprise is the end customer.

| Public proposition | Defensible interpretation | Not established |
|---|---|---|
| Google was an early Globant customer | Google purchased some Globant service | project, buyer, price, team, outcome |
| Filing lists Google among customers | relationship existed in the span represented by the filing | current materiality or continuity since first work |
| Google Cloud partnership | joint market/delivery ecosystem around Google Cloud | Google is the buyer in each cloud engagement |

## Why retain a thin case?

The requested customer has insufficient reliable public detail for a substantial delivery narrative. Rather than substitute a generic story under a famous name, this chapter treats the limitation as the lesson. Provider–platform relationships can have multiple directions: Globant may supply engineering to a technology company, buy or use its platform, implement that platform for third parties, and co-market. “Works with Google” does not identify which economic relationship applies.

A reasonable inference is that winning an early major technology customer provided a young Globant with credibility and exposure to demanding engineering conditions. The sources do not establish that effect, however, nor the work that produced it. It remains an inference, not corporate history.

## What a technology company might buy—and what we cannot say Google did

A mature technology company may use a services firm for elastic capacity, a specialized domain, regional execution, product work, or operational support. These are general purchasing rationales. Without account evidence, none should be assigned to Google. The public record also does not reveal whether work was staff augmentation, a managed team, or scoped delivery.

The Google case establishes an evidentiary discipline that applies throughout Part II: do not convert customer status into project facts, or partner status into customer status. The richer cases that follow show what becomes possible when sources name the business objective and delivery surface.

## Questions to Think About

1. What evidence would distinguish a continuous customer relationship from separate episodes years apart?
2. How could a provider benefit from a platform partnership without the platform company buying services?
3. Why might the most prestigious logo be less educational than a modest but technically documented case?
4. Which project facts would be necessary before drawing conclusions about specialized talent or account expansion?
