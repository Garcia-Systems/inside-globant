## Pattern 1: the deliverable is often a product system

Across sports, travel, media, and automotive evidence, the visible artifact is a channel or platform embedded in an ongoing operation. That makes release, measurement, integration, and evolution as important as initial design. “Website” can badly understate the backend and organizational system needed to keep a journey working.

## Pattern 2: customer experience is integration wearing a human face

A smooth passenger, fan, guest, or buyer journey crosses records and vendors. The experience layer creates demand for API, identity, data, testing, and operational work. Design and engineering are complements: design defines coherent behavior; engineering makes that behavior reliable across the estate.

## Pattern 3: longevity is a different kind of outcome evidence

The filing's long-standing customers cannot tell us what any individual project achieved. They can tell us that some sophisticated customers purchased again over time. That is portfolio evidence of relationship durability, not proof that every account followed a common land-and-expand playbook.

## Pattern 4: announcements and case studies answer different questions

An official announcement is strong evidence of parties, date, intent, and sometimes duration. A case study may provide richer delivery detail and metrics but is usually provider-selected. The best reconstruction joins them. Where only one exists, the limitations column is part of the result.

## Pattern 5: “digital” conceals distinct demand

The sample contains product creation, journey redesign, integration, platform modernization, capacity, and partner-enabled implementation. Their staffing and risk differ. Treating all as digital transformation would discard the operational lesson.

## Questions to Think About

1. Which comparison-table rows support conclusions about delivery, and which support only relationship existence?
2. Why does customer experience repeatedly create backend integration work?
3. What selection bias is introduced by relying on published customer stories?
4. Which additional source would most improve the weakest row?
