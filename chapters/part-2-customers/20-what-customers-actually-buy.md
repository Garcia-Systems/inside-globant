# 20 — What Customers Actually Buy

No customer orders “transformation” for its own sake. The cases suggest six more concrete purchases. The categories overlap because enterprise work is assembled, not boxed.

| Purchase | What the customer receives | People and technical capability | Sought business value | Case signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| product team and throughput | a working increment or continuing delivery capacity | product, design, frontend/backend, QA, DevOps | speed and portfolio throughput | Formula 1, EA |
| specialized expertise | practiced capability unavailable or scarce at the needed moment | gaming, finance, venue, cloud/data or industry specialists | reduce learning time and delivery risk | EA, Santander |
| journey redesign plus implementation | researched service behavior embodied in software | research, service/UX design, application engineering, analytics | adoption, conversion, reduced friction | Nissan, Santander |
| integration | components made to behave as an end-to-end system | architecture, APIs, identity, data, test and observability | reliable operation across seams | Intuit Dome, Nissan |
| modernization | an existing product/platform changed while service continues | cloud/platform, data migration, security, release engineering | adaptability, resilience, lower constraint | cross-case pattern; scope varies |
| enduring delivery relationship | retained context and access to multiple capabilities | account, product, architecture, Studio and delivery leadership | repeated change without restarting trust | EA and filing account metrics |

The table separates what is supported by the sample from a universal claim. An individual statement of work may buy only a subset.

## From label to work

“Customer experience transformation” becomes journey research, prioritized behavior, interfaces, services, telemetry, and release decisions. “AI/data” should become a named model, data pipeline, decision, control, and measure; the principal cases do not provide enough account-level detail to claim AI as a dominant purchase. “Cloud modernization” should specify which workload, migration, reliability condition, and operating model. Marketing nouns become useful only after translation into work and acceptance criteria.

## Not simply staff augmentation

In staff augmentation, a buyer principally directs individuals and absorbs much coordination. In a scoped or managed product engagement, the provider may carry more responsibility for team composition, methods, outputs, and delivery risk. Public cases seldom disclose the contractual model, so it would be wrong to assign one. The useful distinction is responsibility, not whether humans bill time.

A Globant engagement may still contain capacity. Capacity becomes differentiated when combined with a working team, specialized knowledge, delivery leadership, or accountability for a system outcome. Merely calling capacity a “Studio” does not prove the distinction.

## Not SaaS

SaaS provides a standardized product operated for many customers. Services interpret a customer's particular journey and estate, configure platforms, build missing pieces, integrate vendors, migrate data, and adapt governance. Nissan can license commerce and CRM tools; the customer-specific rules and connections still need engineering. Intuit Dome can buy ticketing and payment products; the event-night experience exists across them.

The trade-off is that services do not arrive with the same standardized economics as SaaS. They require customer participation, decisions, access, and continuing maintenance. Reusable accelerators may reduce work but do not eliminate context.

## Not equivalent to hiring employees

Hiring builds permanent internal capability and preserves context, but takes time and creates a continuing fixed commitment. A service firm can mobilize a multidisciplinary group, add a scarce specialty, or vary capacity. It also introduces coordination costs, commercial overhead, knowledge-transfer risk, and dependency. The rational choice is often a mixed system: internal leaders own product, architecture, risk, and institutional knowledge while external teams add bounded capability.

## What people are actually needed?

Public sources do not show full team rosters. The work implies functions, not confirmed titles: a product voice to prioritize; domain stakeholders; researchers/designers; architects; application, platform, data, and quality engineers; security and operations specialists; delivery leadership; and customer decision-makers. Part III must investigate which appear before signature, which enter after, and who bridges the transition.

## Questions to Think About

1. At what point does added engineering capacity become a managed capability rather than staff augmentation?
2. Which customer responsibilities cannot be purchased from a services firm?
3. Why can buying SaaS increase rather than eliminate integration services?
4. How would you define acceptance criteria for “improve customer experience”?
