# Chapter 8 — The United States Market

North America is Globant's largest disclosed revenue region, and the company maintains US locations and publicly documented relationships with US-based organizations. Its annual report explains revenue by geography based on customer location and should be used for the period-specific proportion; the locations directory lists current public addresses ([FY2024 Form 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/browse/?CIK=1557860&owner=exclude), [locations](https://www.globant.com/contact)). “North America” must not be silently relabeled “United States”: it includes more than one country.

## Why the market matters

The United States contains a dense population of large enterprises and technology platforms with substantial transformation budgets. For a global engineering organization, access to those buyers can sustain specialist depth and delivery scale. But high spending attracts intense competition from global consultancies, offshore providers, agencies, platform specialists, contractors, and internal teams.

Public evidence of US-market development includes Globant's NYSE listing, US locations, acquisitions such as WAE, customer/partner announcements, and sponsorships. The WAE announcement explicitly framed the acquisition as adding digital strategy and design and strengthening US presence ([WAE announcement](https://www.globant.com/news/globant-acquires-leading-digital-strategy-and-design-firm)). Globant also publicly describes relationships with technology partners including Google Cloud and AWS; a partner announcement establishes intended collaboration, not the revenue it generated ([Google Cloud announcement](https://www.globant.com/news/globant-expands-partnership-google-cloud), [AWS partner profile](https://partners.amazonaws.com/partners/0010h00001h3VppAAE/Globant)).

## More than remote engineering

Talented distributed developers cannot by themselves perform all work needed to sell to an American enterprise. A provider needs some combination of:

* executive access and account continuity;
* industry and procurement fluency;
* discovery and solution credibility;
* contracts, privacy, security, insurance, and regulatory controls;
* delivery leadership able to bridge client and remote teams;
* partner relationships and local references.

This list describes **general requirements of enterprise services**, supported by the kinds of risks and capabilities in Globant's filing. It is not a published Globant organization chart.

## A two-sided interface

The US-facing organization must translate in both directions. Toward the customer, it turns a vast talent network into a specific, low-risk proposition. Toward delivery, it converts business context into priorities, constraints, decisions, and feedback. Overpromising to the customer burdens delivery; underrepresenting delivery capability loses opportunities. Customer-facing technical work is valuable precisely because it must maintain both truths.

Public customer examples include the FIFA relationship (an international body with substantial US relevance through the 2026 World Cup) and US sports properties, while Globant's filing provides a more reliable aggregate picture than anecdotes. We do not infer customer-level US revenue, staffing locations, or sales ownership from those announcements.

**Unknown:** reviewed public sources do not disclose a complete US reporting structure, sales territories, quotas, account assignment, presales-to-delivery ratios, or which distributed centers serve a particular US client.

## Enterprise credibility has several layers

A provider entering a US enterprise may need a recognized reference to obtain attention, a relevant specialist to survive technical scrutiny, security and legal controls to enter procurement, and a delivery record to expand. Sponsorship can improve awareness; a cloud partnership can validate credentials; an acquisition can add local leaders and relationships. None substitutes for the others, and none alone proves a productive account.

This layered view explains why acquisition of an upstream strategy or design firm may matter. Business sponsors often articulate problems before those problems become engineering scope. People able to facilitate that conversation can bring the global delivery organization closer to the point where priorities are formed. The acquisition announcement documents that strategic intent in the WAE case; whether it generated particular engagements must be established separately.

## Cross-border sales engineering as boundary work

Someone connecting a US buyer with distributed teams must operate across business, technical, organizational, and national boundaries. They must recognize a genuine problem, resist proposing unavailable expertise, make remote capability credible, surface data and regulatory constraints, and preserve context after signature. Fluency in architecture is necessary but insufficient; so are commercial judgment and the ability to create trust without concealing uncertainty.

For a smaller firm, the lesson is not to imitate Globant's office map. It is to make the boundary explicit: identify who owns customer context, who validates feasibility and staffing, and how decisions reach engineers. Globant is the case study; this principle remains an inference from the economics rather than a disclosed Globant playbook.
