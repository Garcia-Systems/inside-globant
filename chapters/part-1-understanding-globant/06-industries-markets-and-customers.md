# Chapter 6 — Industries, Markets, and Customers

Globant's filing discusses customers across media and entertainment, consumer/retail, financial services, travel, technology, professional services, and other sectors. ## What a logo proves—and does not

A reliable announcement or case page establishes at least a public association and the scope the parties chose to describe. It may also document named outputs or reported metrics. It does not necessarily establish current revenue, exclusivity, contract duration, profitability, total relationship scope, team geography, or that Globant caused every outcome.

This distinction prevents “logo collecting,” a common services-industry analytical error. A famous customer may represent a pilot or a major account; without disclosure, we do not know. Conversely, a less visible relationship may be economically material.

## Markets are combinations, not vertical lists

An opportunity sits at an intersection: an industry process, an executive objective, an installed technology estate, a regulatory environment, a geography, and a moment of urgency. Globant's reusable capability must be adapted to that intersection. The customer-facing function identifies what transfers from prior work and what is genuinely specific.

The [customer index](../../appendix/customer-index.md) records the relationship at the narrowest defensible level and flags candidates for Part II. It deliberately avoids unverified lists copied from third parties.

## From market map to account hypothesis

An industry map is useful when it produces testable questions. In an airline, for example, customer-facing channels connect to inventory, pricing, identity, payments, airport operations, and disruption handling. An experience redesign that ignores those systems is decoration; a backend modernization that ignores passenger behavior may not improve the journey. A provider needs both an industry hypothesis and technical discovery.

The same reasoning changes the composition of customer-facing expertise. A regulated bank opportunity may require security, data-governance, and platform voices early. A fan platform may emphasize product, experience, content, and peak-scale engineering. Globant's breadth means those voices are potentially available. It does not prove they were present in any named case unless the evidence says so.

## A customer-evidence ladder

Evidence grows stronger as independent perspectives and specificity accumulate: a provider logo; a provider story; a dated joint announcement; a customer statement naming work; technical artifacts or talks; a measured result with definition and baseline; and repeat evidence over time. Contract documents would add commercial specificity, though confidentiality often makes them unavailable. Part II will locate each relationship on this ladder rather than awarding credibility merely because the customer is famous.

This method also permits negative findings. If only a provider page survives, the defensible account may be short. Restraint is more educational than converting a thin source into a smooth but invented narrative.

## Questions to Think About

1. Why is an industry label alone insufficient for selecting a delivery team?
2. What questions would you ask before treating a public customer logo as evidence of a strategic account?
3. How can a provider reuse experience without exposing confidential client knowledge?
4. Which intersections of industry and technology are likely to require the most customer-facing technical judgment?
