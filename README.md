# Inside Globant
## How a Global Technology Services Company Connects Business Problems with Global Engineering Talent

*Inside Globant* is a research-driven textbook about Globant and, through that case, the global technology-services industry. It is not investment research, a corporate history commissioned by Globant, or an executable course. It contains no application, simulation, or coding exercises.

The book's central question is:

> **How does a global technology-services company turn distributed engineering capability into valuable engagements with major businesses?**

Globant is unusually useful for studying that question. It began in Argentina, became a New York Stock Exchange-listed company, built a multinational delivery footprint, and describes its capabilities through “Studios” rather than only through staffing categories. Its public filings expose the economics; its customer stories expose selected outcomes; neither fully exposes how an individual opportunity is sold and delivered. That combination lets us learn while practicing evidentiary restraint.

## What the book tries to explain

Part I asks what Globant sells and how its delivery machine works. Part II reverses the lens: documented customer relationships show what was built, what value was sought, and what public evidence still cannot tell us about winning the work. Later parts will investigate how deals happen, study partners, and derive bounded lessons for smaller firms.

The recurring mechanism is:

```text
business problem → commercial relationship → solution definition
→ global engineering capability → delivered business outcome
```

The audience is an experienced software engineer who already understands systems but is learning professional services, consulting, enterprise buying, nearshore/offshore delivery, account management, and solution consulting. Technical terms are therefore used directly; commercial terms are explained.

## Evidence policy

Every important contemporary company claim should have an inline link. The [source register](sources/source-register.md) records what each source actually establishes; the [bibliography](appendix/bibliography.md) provides a compact reading list; and [research notes](sources/research-notes.md) preserve uncertainties rather than silently filling them with industry convention. Sources were accessed on **2026-08-11** unless noted. The audited FY2024 Form 20-F is the common quantitative baseline because it provides a coherent period; a later press release should not be mixed into that baseline without saying so.

The text uses three labels:

* **Documented fact** — the cited source directly supports the proposition.
* **Reasonable inference** — evidence supports the interpretation, but Globant has not publicly confirmed it in those terms.
* **Unknown** — public evidence reviewed for this edition does not establish the answer.

Customer stories are evidence that a relationship and described work were publicized—not proof of contract value, the customer's sole motivation, the complete delivery team, or a repeatable internal sales process. Marketing pages establish how Globant presents an offering; they do not independently prove results. General services economics is explicitly separated from Globant disclosures.

## Five-part structure

1. **[Understanding the Globant Machine](chapters/part-1-understanding-globant/00-the-globant-question.md)** — completed here: history, offer, demand, delivery, Studios, customers, economics, the United States, and a bounded end-to-end model.
2. **[Follow the Customers](chapters/part-2-customers/11-follow-the-customers.md)** — customer-by-customer reconstructions and a synthesis of what buyers actually purchase.
3. **[How Deals Happen](chapters/part-3-how-deals-happen/23-the-commercial-engine.md)** — evidence-bounded investigation of relationships, discovery, solution definition, commercials, governance, and expansion.
4. **The Partner Ecosystem** — future study of cloud, platform, and other partners in opportunity creation and delivery.
5. **Lessons for Small Firms** — future synthesis for firms pursuing cross-border, customer-facing technical work without pretending Globant's scale can be copied wholesale.

Parts I–III are written in this release. The empty Part IV–V directories are intentional placeholders, not missing chapters.

## How to read

Read Chapters 0–2 to replace the vague label “outsourcer” with an offer model. Chapters 3–5 explain why buyers engage a firm and how distributed specialization may answer demand. Chapters 6–8 add market and economic constraints. Chapters 9–10 assemble the chain and identify what remains unknown. Follow links when a distinction matters; use the [glossary](appendix/glossary.md), [timeline](appendix/company-timeline.md), and [customer index](appendix/customer-index.md) as maps, not substitutes for source context.
