# About This Book

[Inside Globant](README.md) · [Start Here](START-HERE.md) · [Full Contents](CONTENTS.md)

This book reconstructs an understandable picture of Globant from public evidence. It reads regulatory filings alongside company materials, customer and technology-partner publications, public role descriptions, and independent reporting. Sources do different jobs, so the text does not treat a marketing case study as equivalent to an audited filing or a customer statement.

## Four Evidence Labels

> **Documented fact:** A cited public source directly supports the statement. The source may still have a point of view, date, and limited scope.

> **Reasonable inference:** Public facts support an interpretation, but no source directly discloses the complete mechanism. The text explains the reasoning and avoids presenting it as company policy.

> **General industry model:** A conceptual model describes how professional-services work can operate. It helps readers ask better questions; it is not a claim about Globant's proprietary process.

> **Unknown:** The available evidence does not answer the question reliably. Naming the gap is more useful than filling it with convention or speculation.

## What Public Research Cannot Show

Public information can establish company-level economics, stated capabilities, selected relationships, and some reported outcomes. It cannot reconstruct every confidential negotiation, price, internal sales stage, staffing decision, contract term, or undisclosed account relationship. Even a detailed customer announcement rarely reveals the full statement of work or each party's contribution.

The book therefore treats its reconstruction as an evidence-bounded case study rather than Globant's complete commercial playbook. Readers who need claim-level traceability can use the [source register](sources/source-register.md); unresolved questions remain visible in [Evidence Gaps](sources/evidence-gaps.md).
