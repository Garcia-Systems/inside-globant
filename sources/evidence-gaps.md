# Evidence-Gap Register

Public research can explain a great deal about a company such as Globant, but it cannot reconstruct confidential negotiations, pricing, internal sales stages, or undisclosed account relationships.

Recording those boundaries strengthens the analysis: it separates what public evidence can establish from questions that only transaction participants could answer.

Public evidence can establish the outside of Globant's model more reliably than its internal choreography. These gaps prevent analytical reconstructions from becoming invented company facts.

| Question | Why it matters | Public evidence searched | What remains unknown | Evidence that would resolve it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What are Globant's exact sales stages? | Stage gates determine investment and forecast discipline. | Form 20-F; services pages; public roles; partner and customer announcements | Vocabulary, gates, required artifacts, and exceptions | Current internal playbook plus interviews with process owners |
| Who owns an opportunity at each stage? | Ownership affects customer continuity and promise authority. | Public role descriptions; careers material; filings | Decision rights among business development, client partners, Studios, architects, delivery, and finance | Organization/RACI documents and corroborated practitioner interviews |
| How are commercial roles compensated? | Incentives can encourage qualification, expansion, or overpromising. | Filings and public role material | Quotas, commissions, credit splits, accelerators, and non-sales incentives | Current compensation plans and role-holder interviews |
| How are services priced? | Pricing allocates uncertainty and affects feasibility. | Filing disclosures of time-and-materials and fixed-price work | Rate cards, discount authority, contingencies, and value-based elements | Approved pricing policy and anonymized proposals |
| What are the approval thresholds for deals? | Approval is a control between ambition and delivery risk. | Risk, contract, and governance disclosures | Margin, liability, staffing, security, and executive approval thresholds | Approval matrix and representative deal records |
| What margins do individual projects earn? | Company margin cannot explain account or project economics. | Consolidated financial statements and segment disclosures | Project/account gross margin, presales cost, and remediation cost | Anonymized project P&Ls with allocation methodology |
| How does Globant reserve people before signature? | Capacity credibility affects both win probability and utilization. | Workforce and talent-risk disclosures; role pages | Reservation rules, bench decisions, and conflict resolution | Staffing-system records and delivery-operations interviews |
| What is the standard sales-to-delivery handoff? | Lost assumptions create delivery and trust risk. | Contract/project-risk disclosures; public cases | Required artifacts, attendees, acceptance gates, and escalation | Handoff templates and observed engagements |
| How are partner-originated opportunities attributed? | A directory listing does not establish commercial contribution. | Vendor directories; alliance announcements; marketplace/program pages | Lead volume, referral/co-sell attribution, fees, and partner-sourced revenue | Joint pipeline data and agreements from both parties |
| What happened in specific customer procurement negotiations? | Public cases generally begin after the buying decision. | Customer/provider announcements, cases, and filings | Alternatives considered, objections, security findings, concessions, and decision criteria | Buyer and provider records plus interviews with both sides |
| Which party caused a reported customer outcome? | Software, customer operations, adoption, and external events interact. | Provider case studies and available customer statements | Baseline method, counterfactual, contribution, and durability | Customer-owned measurement, evaluation design, and underlying data |
| Are named customer relationships still active? | Historical evidence should not imply a current contract. | Latest filings, current directories, newsrooms, and case pages | Current scope, renewal status, exclusivity, and economics unless expressly announced | Dated confirmation from the customer and provider |
| What authority does a Studio have? | A capability taxonomy is not an organization chart. | Studio directory, filing descriptions, and careers pages | Budget, staffing, sales, delivery, and P&L authority by Studio | Current governance documents and leader interviews |
| How widely are Globant's AI initiatives deployed? | Announced offers and partnerships do not prove adoption or ROI. | AI service pages; partner announcements; filings | Customer-level usage, model mix, production scale, safety results, and returns | Bounded customer evidence and audited operational measures |

## Research discipline

An unknown is not an invitation to substitute “industry practice.” Where a general model is useful, the manuscript labels it and tests it against disclosed constraints. Future editions should close a row only with evidence that answers the precise question, not merely with another marketing statement.
