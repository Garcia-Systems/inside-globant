# Key Lessons

These lessons are the book's conclusion index. Each is a proposition developed in the linked chapters, not a slogan or a claim that every services firm uses the same organization.

## 1. The Business

1. **Engineering capacity and market access are different capabilities.** A firm can possess one without the other. [Chapters 1](../chapters/part-1-understanding-globant/01-from-argentina-to-global-company.md), [24](../chapters/part-3-how-deals-happen/24-getting-into-the-room.md), and [57](../chapters/part-5-lessons-for-small-firms/57-the-us-market-gap.md).
2. **The engagement is the useful economic unit.** It joins a customer problem, commitments, people, governance, and a hoped-for outcome. [Chapters 0](../chapters/part-1-understanding-globant/00-the-globant-question.md) and [2](../chapters/part-1-understanding-globant/02-what-globant-actually-sells.md).
3. **A service category is not a scope.** “Cloud,” “AI,” and “experience” become purchasable only through bounded outputs, assumptions, and responsibilities. [Chapters 2](../chapters/part-1-understanding-globant/02-what-globant-actually-sells.md) and [34](../chapters/part-3-how-deals-happen/34-from-solution-to-commercial-proposal.md).
4. **Global delivery trades access to talent and resilience for coordination cost.** Geography adds value only when the operating model manages handoffs, context, security, and accountability. [Chapter 4](../chapters/part-1-understanding-globant/04-the-global-delivery-model.md).
5. **Specialization has commercial value when it changes decisions or delivery.** A Studio name or partner badge alone is not evidence of available expertise. [Chapters 5](../chapters/part-1-understanding-globant/05-the-studio-and-specialization-model.md) and [50](../chapters/part-4-partner-ecosystem/50-certifications-specialization-and-trust.md).

## 2. The Customer

6. **Customer motives cannot be read directly from provider capability.** Build-versus-buy depends on speed, scarcity, uncertainty, management capacity, and risk—not price alone. [Chapter 3](../chapters/part-1-understanding-globant/03-why-companies-hire-globant.md).
7. **Reported outcomes need attribution discipline.** A launch announcement states intent; a provider case reports a selected account; neither automatically proves independent causation. [Chapters 11](../chapters/part-2-customers/11-follow-the-customers.md) and [20](../chapters/part-2-customers/20-what-customers-actually-buy.md).
8. **Long relationships are portfolio evidence, not a disclosed expansion script.** Durable accounts show that repeat demand exists while leaving the sequence and economics private. [Chapters 13](../chapters/part-2-customers/13-electronic-arts.md) and [21](../chapters/part-2-customers/21-from-project-to-strategic-account.md).

## 3. The Commercial–Technical Bridge

9. **Trust is produced through evidence and behavior.** References, truthful estimates, security controls, delivery recovery, and continuity reduce different uncertainties. [Chapters 25](../chapters/part-3-how-deals-happen/25-accounts-relationships-and-trust.md) and [58](../chapters/part-5-lessons-for-small-firms/58-the-trust-gap.md).
10. **Discovery is decision work, not a ceremonial meeting.** It identifies the affected workflow, consequence, stakeholder, constraints, and unknowns before solution commitment. [Chapters 29](../chapters/part-3-how-deals-happen/29-discovery.md) and [59](../chapters/part-5-lessons-for-small-firms/59-the-discovery-gap.md).
11. **Technical presales reduces promise risk.** Technical and delivery judgment must reach the commercial decision before scope and price harden. [Chapters 30](../chapters/part-3-how-deals-happen/30-the-technical-commercial-bridge.md)–[33](../chapters/part-3-how-deals-happen/33-scoping-estimation-and-feasibility.md).
12. **Title and function are not the same.** Sales engineer, solutions engineer, architect, and presales consultant may overlap, but their authority and work must be established rather than assumed. [Chapters 27](../chapters/part-3-how-deals-happen/27-client-partners-and-account-leadership.md), [30](../chapters/part-3-how-deals-happen/30-the-technical-commercial-bridge.md), and the [glossary](glossary.md).
13. **Procurement readiness is part of the offer.** A feasible solution still needs an acceptable entity, contract, security posture, risk allocation, and operating evidence. [Chapter 35](../chapters/part-3-how-deals-happen/35-enterprise-buying-and-procurement.md).
14. **The sale-to-delivery boundary is a control point.** Assumptions, decisions, customer duties, and unresolved risks must survive handoff. [Chapter 38](../chapters/part-3-how-deals-happen/38-when-sales-becomes-delivery.md).
15. **Strong delivery can become a commercial asset, but expansion must be requalified.** A successful project earns evidence and permission, not entitlement to more scope. [Chapters 37](../chapters/part-3-how-deals-happen/37-land-expand-and-grow-the-account.md) and [67](../chapters/part-5-lessons-for-small-firms/67-from-first-deal-to-repeatable-model.md).
16. **A technology sale can create implementation demand.** Configuration, integration, migration, governance, and adoption surround platforms; the existence of demand does not guarantee it flows to a particular partner. [Chapters 48](../chapters/part-4-partner-ecosystem/48-how-platforms-create-services-work.md) and [49](../chapters/part-4-partner-ecosystem/49-co-selling-and-routes-to-market.md).
17. **“Partner” names a relationship category, not a commercial result.** Platform provider, alliance, directory membership, joint offering, investment, and customer relationship require separate evidence. [Chapters 41](../chapters/part-4-partner-ecosystem/41-the-ecosystem-around-globant.md) and [52](../chapters/part-4-partner-ecosystem/52-what-part-iv-teaches-us.md).

## 4. The Smaller-Firm Implication

18. **A company is not a sales opportunity.** A real opportunity has a problem, impact, owner, urgency, plausible external fit, budget path, and decision process. [Chapter 65](../chapters/part-5-lessons-for-small-firms/65-choosing-the-right-us-opportunity.md).
19. **Local presence is conditional, not universally required.** It matters when it improves access, discovery, trust, coordination, or accountability enough to justify its cost. [Chapter 61](../chapters/part-5-lessons-for-small-firms/61-the-local-presence-question.md).
20. **An external commercial or technical representative is one allocation option, not a shortcut.** It works only with credible delivery, aligned incentives, explicit authority, real customer demand, and viable economics. [Chapters 62](../chapters/part-5-lessons-for-small-firms/62-the-independent-sales-engineering-model.md) and [63](../chapters/part-5-lessons-for-small-firms/63-how-the-economics-might-work.md).
21. **A smaller firm need not reproduce Globant's organization.** It must ensure that essential functions are performed, while combining, focusing, or externalizing them according to its market and risk. [Chapters 53](../chapters/part-5-lessons-for-small-firms/53-shrinking-the-globant-model.md), [56](../chapters/part-5-lessons-for-small-firms/56-what-small-firms-cannot-copy.md), and [68](../chapters/part-5-lessons-for-small-firms/68-the-globant-at-100-people-model.md).
22. **The complete mechanism is a feedback system.** Market access, discovery, translation, solution design, commercial commitment, delivery, outcome evidence, and account learning reinforce—or undermine—one another. [Chapters 9](../chapters/part-1-understanding-globant/09-the-commercial-technical-delivery-chain.md) and [69](../chapters/part-5-lessons-for-small-firms/69-final-lessons-from-globant.md).
