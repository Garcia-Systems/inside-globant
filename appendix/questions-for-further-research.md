# Questions for Further Research

Public evidence reviewed through **2026-08-11** could not answer these questions reliably.

## Globant

1. How are opportunity ownership, presales staffing and approval rights distributed by region and account?
2. What proportion of opportunities originate with clients, Globant teams, acquisitions or technology partners?
3. How are discovery and solution work priced, staffed and handed into delivery?
4. What are win rates, sales-cycle distributions, acquisition costs and alliance-attributed revenue?
5. Which role combinations differ between new logos, established accounts and procurement-led bids?

## Smaller firms

6. Which U.S. buyer segments accept fully remote foreign providers, and under what risk conditions?
7. When does a local employee outperform founder travel, an independent partner or vendor channel?
8. How do results differ among referral, commission, retainer and hybrid representation arrangements?
9. What minimum security, insurance, financial and entity evidence do buyers require by deal type?
10. How much protected senior-engineering presales capacity is economically sustainable at 25, 100 and 250 people?
11. Which discovery activities buyers will pay for before a first cross-border engagement?
12. How often does a first U.S. project become referenceable and expand, controlling for selection bias?
13. Which failure modes most damage the customer, provider and intermediary—and which recovery practices work?
14. How do AI-assisted development and changing procurement/security rules alter small-provider advantage?

## Research design

Useful evidence would include anonymized CRM cohorts, buyer and lost-prospect interviews, contract samples, time studies, delivery outcome data, representative agreements, and matched comparisons across geography and work model. Publication should protect confidentiality and separate correlation from causation.
