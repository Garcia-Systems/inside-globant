# Partner Evaluation Framework

Use this before placing a personal or corporate reputation in front of an engineering provider. It is not legal, security, tax, accounting, or investment advice.

## Evidence gates

Score **verified / partly verified / absent / not applicable**, attach evidence, owner and review date, and record severity rather than collapsing everything into one number.

1. **Identity:** entity, ownership, leaders, locations, contracting authority, sanctions/conflicts checks appropriate to the parties.
2. **Financial continuity:** statements or proportionate evidence, concentration, cash-cycle tolerance, insurance, business continuity.
3. **Specialty:** precise claims, comparable work, actual contribution, named practitioners, honest limits.
4. **References:** customer permission, relevance, delivery behavior, recovery behavior, team continuity.
5. **Technical judgment:** architecture exercise, tradeoffs, integration/data/security reasoning, explicit unknowns.
6. **Estimation:** basis, assumptions, ranges, dependencies, validation, change control and historical variance.
7. **Delivery:** accountable lead, planning, quality, testing, releases, observability, reporting, escalation.
8. **Security/privacy:** scoped controls, identity/access, devices, SDLC, incidents, data handling, subprocessors, assurance boundaries.
9. **People/capacity:** proposed team, availability, employment/subcontract model, overlap, attrition, replacements.
10. **Communication:** live and written clarity, disagreement, bad-news behavior, decision records.
11. **Commercial readiness:** IP, confidentiality, payment, acceptance, liability, termination, exit and counsel review.
12. **Presales partnership:** shared qualification, authority boundaries, cost, proposal approval, handoff and account ownership.

## Practical tests

- Conduct two reference calls, including one involving difficulty.
- Run a paid or mutually bounded exercise representative of the main risk—not a toy.
- Ask the proposed delivery lead to critique the draft proposal.
- Trace one promise from customer statement through architecture, estimate, contract and delivery control.
- Simulate an incident and a material scope discovery.
- Verify certificates/reports at their issuer and inspect scope, date and exceptions.

## Warning signs

- an impressive website but no verifiable, relevant case or reference;
- a sales team that cannot provide accountable technical leadership before commitment;
- chronic underestimation, unexplained estimate variance, or schedules with no assumptions;
- poor written communication or material differences between verbal and written promises;
- unclear ownership of scope, architecture, delivery, escalation, or the contracting entity;
- unwillingness to discuss a failure, incident, difficult customer, or recovery;
- critical dependency on one engineer with no continuity plan;
- hidden subcontracting or a proposed team that never meets the buyer;
- no repeatable contract and proposal review process;
- certificates used to avoid answering questions about actual control scope; and
- universal expertise, guaranteed outcomes, or other unrealistic promises.

## Decision record

Record: intended opportunity class; unacceptable risks; remediation owners/dates; permitted claims; maximum initial engagement; required approvals; review date; and termination triggers. Reassess after leadership, ownership, capacity, incident or quality changes.
