# Glossary

Definitions are used in this book; firms may use the same words differently.

**Account expansion** — additional work won with an existing customer, whether in the same program, capability, business unit, or geography.

**Account management** — the function of sustaining customer context, relationships, commercial health, and coordinated response across engagements. It is not assumed to be a specific Globant title.

**Billable work** — effort chargeable under a client agreement. A firm's accounting definition may differ from a manager's utilization definition.

**Business development** — activity that creates, develops, or qualifies commercial opportunities.

**Channel** — a route through another organization by which a product or service reaches a customer; it may involve referral, resale, distribution, marketplace procurement, or services, so the exact arrangement must be specified.

**Cloud partner** — a company recognized within a cloud provider's ecosystem to provide one or more services, solutions, software, resale, or implementation functions; the vendor's current program defines the scope.

**Co-selling** — coordinated selling by two companies around complementary offerings. It does not by itself imply a common contract, shared revenue, referral payment, or equal account control.

**Competency** — a vendor-program designation intended to recognize defined organizational capability or experience. Criteria, validity, and scope depend on the vendor and period.

**Commercial engine** — the connected capabilities that turn market access and customer problems into feasible, purchasable, deliverable engagements; not a claimed Globant department or proprietary method.

**Commercial proposal** — an offer that translates a solution into commitments about scope, deliverables, team, timing, responsibilities, assumptions, price and terms.

**Client / customer concentration** — the portion of revenue associated with one client or a small group; scale in an account can create efficiency and dependency risk.

**Consulting** — structured diagnosis and advice about choices, operating implications, and change; it may be paired with implementation.

**Delivery center** — a location organized to perform client work. An office address alone does not establish that role.

**Digital transformation** — a portfolio of changes to propositions, experiences, decisions, processes, organization, and enabling technology; not a discrete deliverable.

**Discovery** — investigation of objectives, users, processes, constraints, systems, data, risks, and measures before and during solution definition.

**Distributed delivery** — performance of an engagement by people in more than one location.

**Fixed price** — a commercial form in which an agreed price is associated with defined scope or deliverables, transferring more estimation/scope risk to the provider than straightforward time-and-materials work.

**General industry model** — an explanatory model of common professional-services activity that is not presented as documented Globant practice.

**Hyperscaler** — a provider operating cloud infrastructure and managed services at very large, elastic scale; commonly used for major global cloud providers.

**Implementation partner** — a services organization that configures, builds, integrates, migrates, tests, deploys, or operates technology for a customer. The label alone does not establish every activity.

**ISV (independent software vendor)** — a company whose business centers on developing and distributing software for multiple customers, rather than software solely for its own internal use.

**Marketplace** — a vendor-operated catalog and procurement route for software or services. A listing does not establish that a transaction occurred.

**Glober** — Globant's term for a member of its workforce; use in a filing may have a defined period and scope.

**Gross margin** — revenue less cost of revenues, divided by revenue. It is not operating margin and does not include every cost of running the company.

**Nearshore** — cross-border delivery from a relatively proximate geography, commonly with meaningful working-hour overlap.

**Offshore** — cross-border delivery from a more distant geography; the boundary with nearshore is contextual.

**Onshore / onsite** — respectively, provider work within the customer's country / work at or directly alongside a customer location.

**Professional services** — expertise and organized labor applied to a client's specific situation, rather than only access to a standardized product.

**Platform ecosystem** — the vendors, services firms, developers, customers, programs, extensions, and commercial routes that form around a reusable technology platform.

**Sales engineering / solution consulting** — customer-facing technical functions that help discover needs, demonstrate credibility, test feasibility, and shape a solution. This book does not assume Globant uses either title universally.

**Technical–commercial bridge** — the person or distributed function that translates customer objectives into engineering implications and technical realities back into business decisions; not necessarily a formal title or department.

**Solution architecture** — translation of objectives and constraints into a feasible technical structure and delivery approach.

**Specialization** — focused expertise, or a vendor designation for such expertise, in a technology, workload, industry, or outcome. When capitalized or used as a program term, its vendor-specific criteria should be checked.

**Systems integrator** — a services company that makes multiple products, applications, data sources, and operational processes function as a coherent customer solution.

**Studio** — Globant's public organizing and go-to-market concept for areas of deep industry or technology expertise; public evidence does not make it synonymous with a legal entity, project team, or profit center.

**Time and materials (T&M)** — pricing based primarily on effort/capacity consumed at agreed rates, with scope risk distributed differently from fixed-price work.

**Technology partner** — an organization with a documented relationship involving technology, capability, market activity, or delivery. Because the term is broad, the precise relationship and evidence should always follow it.

**Utilization** — proportion of available delivery capacity applied to qualifying productive or billable work. Always ask for the numerator, denominator, and period.
