# Deal Anatomy

> **CONCEPTUAL PROFESSIONAL-SERVICES MODEL — NOT GLOBANT'S PROPRIETARY PROCESS.**

This appendix compresses [Part III](../chapters/part-3-how-deals-happen/23-the-commercial-engine.md) into a reusable map. Real opportunities skip, repeat and reorder activities; discovery can end a pursuit, and procurement can reopen the solution.

| Lifecycle activity | Question resolved | Minimum useful output | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| presence/access | why will the customer talk? | relevant relationship and mandate | awareness mistaken for trust |
| opportunity formation | is there a material change worth investigating? | problem hypothesis and stakeholders | provider invents demand |
| qualification | should both parties invest further? | fit, urgency, authority, constraints, next decision | momentum without buyability |
| discovery | what is wrong, for whom, and why now? | evidence-based problem definition | request accepted literally |
| technical investigation | what estate and constraints shape options? | dependencies, unknowns and feasibility evidence | hidden integration/security risk |
| solution design | what intervention best connects outcome and system? | options, recommendation, target behavior/architecture | technology disconnected from value |
| delivery validation | can the capability actually be mobilized? | credible skills, leadership, geography and timing | generic capability treated as availability |
| scope/estimate | what work and uncertainty support commitment? | scope, range/estimate, assumptions, dependencies | false precision |
| proposal | what can be purchased? | deliverables, team, timeline, responsibilities, price and terms | aspiration becomes contractual ambiguity |
| enterprise approval | will budget, procurement, legal, security and risk accept it? | resolved diligence and authority | sponsor enthusiasm mistaken for signature |
| negotiation/signature | is risk allocation mutually acceptable? | executed agreement with controlled side commitments | commercial edits invalidate design |
| mobilization/handoff | can delivery reproduce the reasoning behind the sale? | shared charter, decisions, backlog and governance | information cliff |
| delivery/measurement | is useful system behavior being produced? | increments, operational evidence and decisions | output confused with business outcome |
| expansion/close | is there another independently valuable problem? | qualified next opportunity or deliberate end | entitlement and dependency |

## The bidirectional bridge

```text
customer objectives, economics and constraints
                    ↓
        technical–commercial translation
                    ↓
architecture, work, dependencies, risk and options
                    ↑
        technical–commercial translation
                    ↑
customer decision, funding and responsibility
```

## Controls across the lifecycle

1. Label facts, assumptions, inferences and unknowns.
2. Increase specialist involvement as uncertainty and commitment rise.
3. Keep customer responsibilities visible.
4. Match estimate precision to evidence.
5. Revalidate feasibility after material commercial changes.
6. Preserve decision rationale into delivery.
7. Treat delivery behavior as evidence for—not entitlement to—future work.
