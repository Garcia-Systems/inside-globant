# Deal Anatomy

> **CONCEPTUAL PROFESSIONAL-SERVICES MODEL — NOT GLOBANT'S SALES PROCESS.**

This map compresses [Part III](../chapters/part-3-how-deals-happen/23-the-commercial-engine.md). Real pursuits skip, repeat, and reorder stages; discovery may end a pursuit, and buyer review may reopen the solution.

| Stage | Primary question | Commercial concern | Technical concern | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Market / account | Why this market and why may the customer listen? | relevance, access, conflict, acquisition cost | credible capability for target problems | account thesis and permission to engage |
| Opportunity | Is there a consequential change to investigate? | owner, impact, urgency | plausible system/workflow boundary | problem hypothesis and stakeholders |
| Discovery | What is happening, for whom, and why now? | access, decision purpose, paid/unpaid boundary | estate, data, constraints, unknowns | evidence-based problem definition |
| Qualification | Should both parties invest further? | budget path, authority, alternatives, timing | fit, controllable dependencies, unacceptable risk | pursue/pause/decline decision and next test |
| Solution | Which intervention best connects problem and system? | differentiated value and customer duties | options, architecture, security, operability | recommendation with tradeoffs |
| Feasibility | Can the promised capability be mobilized? | timing, risk appetite, partner/subcontract terms | people, platform, proof, capacity | feasibility decision and unresolved risks |
| Scope | What work can responsibly be committed? | acceptance, change, allocation of uncertainty | decomposition, estimates, dependencies | scope, assumptions, estimate/range |
| Commercial proposal | What can the buyer purchase? | price, terms, responsibilities, validity | technical commitments remain coherent | controlled proposal |
| Buyer review | Will sponsors and evaluators support the choice? | business case, alternatives, authority | architecture, security, operations evidence | decision or requested changes |
| Procurement | Is the provider and risk allocation acceptable? | entity, liability, IP, payment, compliance | data access, control scope, continuity | completed diligence and negotiated issues |
| Contract | Are mutual commitments authorized and consistent? | signed terms and controlled side promises | proposal changes revalidated | executed agreement |
| Handoff | Can delivery reproduce the reasoning behind the sale? | ownership, governance, escalation | decisions, backlog, environments, risks | shared charter and readiness decision |
| Delivery | Is useful, accepted behavior being produced? | forecast, change, communication | quality, releases, reliability, security | increments, evidence, and decisions |
| Expansion / close | Is there another independently valuable problem? | reference permission and viable next demand | outcome evidence and continuing fit | qualified next opportunity or deliberate end |

## Controls across the lifecycle

1. Label facts, assumptions, inferences, and unknowns.
2. Increase specialist involvement as uncertainty and commitment rise.
3. Keep customer and third-party responsibilities visible.
4. Match estimate precision to evidence.
5. Revalidate technical feasibility after material commercial changes.
6. Preserve decision rationale into delivery.
7. Treat delivered value as evidence for—not entitlement to—future work.

For the hypothetical cross-border walkthrough, [Chapter 66](../chapters/part-5-lessons-for-small-firms/66-the-first-deal.md) adds explicit customer, representative, and provider responsibilities. Compensation, account ownership, proposal authority, communication, handoff, and escalation should be agreed before a live pursuit.
