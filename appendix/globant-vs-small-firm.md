# Globant vs. Smaller Global Engineering Firms

This synthesis compares capability systems, not company quality. “Globant at scale” is bounded by public evidence; the smaller-firm columns are **general analytical models**, not staffing prescriptions. Externalization can supply a function, but accountability, information flow, and decision authority still need an owner.

| Capability | Globant at scale | 100-person firm | 25-person specialist firm | Can be externalized? | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brand | Public-company identity and named relationships | Narrow, evidence-led reputation | Founder and specialist reputation | Partly—borrowed trust is fragile | Creates consideration but does not prove fit |
| Market access | Locations, leaders, accounts, marketing, acquisitions, alliances | One chosen market and accountable owner | Founder network or tightly bounded channel | Yes, if incentives and conflicts are explicit | Delivery capacity does not create conversations |
| Business development | Multiple regions and marketed sectors; exact structure undisclosed | One focused pipeline discipline | Founder-led, low-volume pursuit | Yes | Finds and tests demand before expensive presales |
| Industry expertise | Multiple marketed industry practices | One or two workflows | One defensible niche | Selectively | Connects technical work to operating consequence |
| Discovery | Consulting/design capabilities are marketed; universal method unknown | Repeatable workshop and paid discovery option | Senior-led diagnosis | Partly; customer access cannot be outsourced away | Turns a request into decision-quality evidence |
| Technical presales | Broad specialist pool and advertised functions | Protected senior architect time | Principal engineer joins qualified pursuits | Selectively | Prevents commercial promises outrunning technical reality |
| Solution architecture | Multidisciplinary capability across Studios/platforms | Named architecture owner | Principal plus specialist adviser | Partly, with continuing accountability | Joins systems, constraints, and delivery choices |
| Specialists | Broad Studio and partner map | Two or three genuine practices | One deep specialty | Yes, with transparent subcontracting | Adds scarce judgment without pretending universal depth |
| Proposal capability | Corporate commercial infrastructure; templates/approvals private | Reusable scope, assumption, price, and review controls | Simple controlled proposal plus counsel | Partly | Makes a solution purchasable and preserves assumptions |
| Procurement readiness | Listed-company legal, finance, security, and geographic infrastructure | Proportionate diligence pack and advisers | Bounded risk posture; decline unsuitable buyers | Much can; contracting entity remains accountable | Enterprise approval can stop technically sound work |
| Delivery | Large distributed workforce and program capacity | Bounded teams with named lead | Senior, concentrated team | Core delivery usually not if it defines the proposition | Produces the evidence on which trust and expansion depend |
| Security and risk | Company-level controls and disclosure; engagement scope varies | Documented controls matched to data/work | Narrow scope and explicit exclusions | Assurance/advice can; responsibility cannot | Buyers evaluate dependency, data, continuity, and recovery |
| Technology partnerships | Multiple documented platform relationships | One or two actively used ecosystems | Certification only where commercially relevant | Alliance management can | Can add capability and routes; badges do not guarantee leads |
| Account management | Long-lived large accounts; internal choreography undisclosed | One owner coordinates commercial and delivery context | Founder or delivery lead with capacity protected | Yes, if authority and continuity are clear | Preserves learning and qualifies subsequent demand |
| Customer references | Portfolio of named relationships and selected cases | Few relevant, permissioned references | One or two specific cases | No—another party may introduce, not transfer evidence | Reduces uncertainty about comparable work and behavior |
| Geographic presence | Client and delivery locations across regions | Designed overlap, travel, local help when justified | Remote-first with explicit limits | Yes, conditionally | Address matters only when it improves access or execution |

## How to use the comparison

Start with the target customer's risk and the problem being sold, not an aspiration to fill every row. A 25-person specialist may need stronger senior continuity than Globant but no broad industry portfolio. A 100-person firm pursuing regulated enterprise programs may need more procurement and security capacity than one selling bounded product engineering to midmarket buyers. “Can be externalized” never means “can be ignored.”
